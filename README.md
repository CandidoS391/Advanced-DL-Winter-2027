## Overview

This is the repository for the Winter 2027 ACM DAS advanced project: (Seeing the World Using Computer Vision/Predicting the Future using Deep Learning. This 8-10 week project introduces participants to the mathematical foundations and practical implementation of deep learning, with an emphasis on neural networks and computer vision. Through guided explanations and interactive demonstrations, participants will explore general deep learning concepts such as neural network architectures, forward propagation, backpropagation, training, regularization, and model evaluation; while also focusing on specific applications of deep learning such as image classification using computer vision and convolutional neural networks, or forecasting future observations from historical time-series data using recurrent neural networks (RNNs) and long short-term memory networks (LSTMs).

After developing these foundations, participants will pursue independent projects in which they select their own datasets, implement neural network models, and investigate questions of their choosing. Participants are encouraged to experiment with different architectures, training strategies, and evaluation methods while developing an understanding of how deep learning models behave in practice.

The project emphasizes curiosity, experimentation, and technical understanding rather than simply achieving high accuracy. It also introduces participants to good scientific practices, including documenting methodologies, interpreting results, and discussing limitations. Although formal research experience is not required and publication is not an expectation, the project can serve as a stepping stone toward more advanced machine learning work and future research opportunities. 

All materials used during project meetings (i.e slides, notebooks), as well as additional resources (challenges, references, and eventually sample reports) are included here based on the listing of the directory (for instance, all slides for the meetings are posted in the slides directory). Essentially, everything you may need while developing your deep learning project will live here, while all communications will go through on the ACM DAS Discord. 

**Warning:** Deep learning is not always going to be an intuitive subject, and some of the concepts presented throughout may be difficult to understand upon initial exposure. Participants are not expected to immediately grasp every concept, as developing intuition for deep learning often requires time, practical experimentation, and independent study. However, participants should not feel obligated to dedicate time to this project at the expense of their coursework, academic commitments, or other responsibilities. Thus, participants are encouraged to ask questions, revisit instructional materials, and consult supplementary resources as needed.

## Prerequisites and Expectations

This project was primarily designed for those who have basic knowledge of machine learning, data science, and programming techniques (i.e the skills built up in the DAS' beginner and intermediate projects). Whilst the project leads will welcome and try to accommodate everyone on all levels of experience, it is with this in mind that they will expect project members to be familiar with the following:
- Programming: Familiarity in at least one programming language, including familiarity with functions, data structures, and basic debugging.
- Data Science: Familiarity with data preprocessing, exploratory data analysis, working with datasets, and interpreting results.
- Machine Learning: Familiarity with fundamental concepts such as supervised learning, training and testing datasets, loss functions, and model evaluation.

Additionally, but not required, it is highly recommend some familiarity with some math concepts vital to deep learning, mainly calculus, linear algebra, and probability/statistics. Underneath the underlying framework, these concepts help power the machinery of neural networks, and so having an elementary understanding of the following certainly helps:
- Linear Algebra: Familiarity with vectors, matrices, matrix multiplication, linear transformations, and basic matrix operations.
- Calculus: Familiarity with derivatives, the chain rule, gradients, and basic multivariable calculus (Partial Derivatives and Gradients).
- Probability and Statistics: Familiarity with probability distributions, expected values, variance, descriptive statistics, and basic statistical analysis.

Finally any preexisting knowledge or work with deep learning is certainly welcomed, but is not expected. Above all, participants are expected to be active in the project, be able to learn new concepts, seek clarification if needed, and most importantly be a positive figure for all in the project.

## Independent Projects and Participation Philosophy

### Project Structure and Creative Freedom
For the Winter 2027 project, participants will have the freedom to design their own model that is trained on a predetermined dataset (TBD by the project lead) while answering an experimental question of their choosing. Although participants will work with a common dataset, they are encouraged to explore different neural network architectures, training strategies, hyperparameters, and data augmentation techniques. Above all, participants are encouraged to explore beyond the examples given during meetings and beyond what is presented in this repository. To facilitate this exploration, optional challenges will be made available throughout the project to explore specific topics in neural network theory and help participants develop ideas for their independent experiments.

### Experimentation and Independent Learning
When developing the model, participants are encouraged to approach their projects with curiosity, creativity, and a willingness to experiment. Instead of focusing solely on achieving high model accuracy, participants should seek to understand how and why their models behave the way they do.

In terms of experimentation, think of modifying neural network architectures, adjusting hyperparameters, exploring different training strategies, or investigating the effects of data preprocessing and augmentation. All of these topics can form a question that can be answered via experimentation, and interpretation from those said experiments, whether or not they succeeded.

In this project, independent learning will be a central component of this project. Whilst the project leads will provide guidance and technical support throughout, participants are encouraged to take ownership of their work, consult external resources, and explore concepts beyond those covered during instructional sessions. This includes collaborating with others, as well as consulting with AI-assisted tools. However, participants should make a reasonable effort to understand their implementations, justify their experimental decisions, and interpret their results.

### Documentation and Sharing Results
Towards the end of the project, participants will be writing up a report that details the work that they've done, what they were trying to accomplish, what were the results of the experiments done, etc. A guide to writing these reports, as well as a few sample reports will be shown in this repository.

Additionally but not required, participants may showcase their work to others in the project, and open themselves up for critique from their peers. *(Note: This hasn't been settled just yet.)*
